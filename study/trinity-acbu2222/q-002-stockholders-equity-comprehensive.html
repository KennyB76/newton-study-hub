Stockholders Equity — Comprehensive

ABC Corp · Treasury + Cumulative Preferred + 3-date Dividends · 5 parts · 2026-05-08

Question

📝 Given — Jan 1, 2026 Stockholders' Equity
Common Stock, $5 par, 100,000 authorized, 50,000 issued and outstanding $250,000 Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par — Common 400,000 8% Cumulative Preferred Stock, $50 par, 10,000 authorized, 5,000 issued and outstanding 250,000 Retained Earnings 800,000 Preferred dividends in arrears: $20,000 (2 years unpaid)
📅 2026 Events
• Mar 1 — Issued 5,000 common @ $20/share • Jun 15 — Repurchased 1,000 common as treasury @ $22 (cost method) • Sep 1 — Reissued 500 treasury @ $25/share • Nov 1 — Declared cash dividends: - Preferred current: $20,000 - Preferred arrears: $20,000 - Common: $0.50/share (on outstanding) - Record: Nov 15 · Payment: Dec 1 • Dec 31 — Net income: $300,000
🎯 Required (5 parts)
  1. (a) Mar 1 journal entry — common stock issuance
  2. (b) Jun 15 journal entry — treasury repurchase (cost method)
  3. (c) Sep 1 journal entry — treasury reissuance
  4. (d) ALL dividend journal entries (Nov 1 + Nov 15 + Dec 1)
  5. (e) Stockholders' Equity section on Dec 31, 2026

Setup — Key Formulas

① Common Stock Issuance Above Par
Cash = Shares × Issue Price
Common Stock = Shares × Par
Paid-in Capital in Excess = Cash − Common Stock
② Treasury Stock — Cost Method
Treasury Stock (debit) = Shares × Repurchase Cost
On reissue: Cash − Cost = Paid-in Cap from Treasury (credit)
If Cash < Cost: debit Paid-in Cap from Treasury, then RE
③ Outstanding Shares (for common dividend)
Outstanding = Issued − Treasury
Track changes through the year, use balance at record date
④ Cumulative Preferred Dividend Priority
Pay Order: Arrears → Current Preferred → Common
Arrears must be cleared first; common gets nothing until preferred is current
(a) Mar 1, 2026 — Common Stock Issuance
5,000 shares × $20 = $100,000 received. Par = $5/share = $25,000. Excess = $15/share = $75,000.
Cash100,000 Common Stock (5,000 × $5)25,000 Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par — Common75,000

After this: Common Stock = $275,000 (55,000 shares issued). PIC Excess = $475,000.

(b) Jun 15, 2026 — Treasury Repurchase
Cost method: Treasury Stock recorded at full repurchase cost. 1,000 × $22 = $22,000.
Treasury Stock22,000 Cash22,000

Treasury Stock is a contra-equity (deducted from total SE). No change to Common Stock account itself — the shares are still issued, just not outstanding.

After this: 55,000 issued, 1,000 treasury, 54,000 outstanding.

(c) Sep 1, 2026 — Treasury Reissuance Above Cost
500 shares × $25 = $12,500 received. Cost basis = 500 × $22 = $11,000. Excess $1,500 → Paid-in Capital from Treasury Stock (credit).
Cash12,500 Treasury Stock (500 × $22)11,000 Paid-in Capital from Treasury Stock1,500

Critical: Reissue gain never goes to Retained Earnings. It always goes to Paid-in Capital from Treasury Stock.

After this: 500 treasury remaining, 54,500 outstanding common.

Common Mistakes

⚠ The 5 traps that cost points on this question type
  • Forgetting cumulative preferred arrears
    Cumulative preferred MUST receive arrears before any common dividend. If you give common $0.50 without clearing arrears, that's wrong.
  • Wrong outstanding share count for common dividend
    Common dividend uses outstanding shares (issued − treasury), not issued. At record date Nov 15: 55,000 − 500 = 54,500.
  • Treasury reissue gain to Retained Earnings
    Excess on reissue NEVER hits RE. Always Paid-in Capital from Treasury Stock (credit).
  • Recording journal entry on record date
    Record date = no journal entry. Many students debit/credit something — wrong. It's just a list of payees.
  • Treasury Stock placement on Balance Sheet
    Treasury is a deduction at the BOTTOM of stockholders' equity, after Retained Earnings. NOT in Paid-in Capital.
💡 Key Takeaway — 30-second review
  • Common Stock always recorded at par. Excess goes to Paid-in Capital in Excess of Par.
  • Treasury Stock (cost method): debit at cost, contra-equity (subtracted from total SE).
  • Treasury reissue above cost: extra → Paid-in Capital from Treasury Stock (credit). Never RE.
  • Cumulative preferred: arrears + current paid before any common dividend.
  • Common dividend: per-share amount × outstanding shares (not issued).
  • 3 dividend dates: Declaration (debit Dividends, credit Payable) → Record (no entry) → Payment (debit Payable, credit Cash).

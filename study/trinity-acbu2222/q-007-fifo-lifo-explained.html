FIFO vs LIFO — Explained Like You're in Middle School

Source: Trinity_Baek / Questions Board / "Help me" (2026-05-09)
Question: "Help me understand fifo and lifo"

The 1-Sentence Version

FIFO and LIFO are two ways to decide which inventory got sold first when you have stuff that came in at different prices over time.

Real-Life Mental Model

🥛 FIFO — "Milk in the Fridge"

First In, First Out

When you buy a new gallon of milk, you push the old one to the front. The old milk gets used first (or it'll go bad). The newest milk stays in the back.

Sold first = oldest stuff
Left over = newest stuff

📚 LIFO — "Stack of Books"

Last In, First Out

When you stack books, you put the new one on top. When you grab one, you take the top one. The bottom books stay buried.

Sold first = newest stuff
Left over = oldest stuff

Why It Matters — Numbers Example

Imagine you sell skateboards. You bought inventory 3 times this year, but prices went UP each time:

Purchase OrderQuantityPrice/eachTotal Cost
Jan — Order 110$50$500
Jun — Order 210$60$600
Nov — Order 310$70$700
Total available30$1,800

You sold 20 skateboards for $100 each → Sales Revenue = $2,000.

Now we need to figure out: which 20 did you sell? That changes COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) and what's left in inventory.

Under FIFO (oldest goes first)

Sold first = the 10 from Jan ($50 each) + the 10 from Jun ($60 each)
COGS = (10 × $50) + (10 × $60) = $500 + $600 = $1,100
Ending Inventory = 10 left from Nov × $70 = $700

Under LIFO (newest goes first)

Sold first = the 10 from Nov ($70 each) + the 10 from Jun ($60 each)
COGS = (10 × $70) + (10 × $60) = $700 + $600 = $1,300
Ending Inventory = 10 left from Jan × $50 = $500

Side-by-Side Comparison

FIFOLIFO
Sales Revenue$2,000$2,000
COGS$1,100$1,300
Gross Profit$900$700
Ending Inventory (on Balance Sheet)$700$500
The Big Insight (when prices are rising):
  • FIFO → lower COGS → higher Net Income → higher Tax → higher Ending Inventory
  • LIFO → higher COGS → lower Net Income → lower Tax → lower Ending Inventory
Mirror image. Same business, different numbers, just because of which method.

Why Companies Choose One or the Other

Companies Using FIFO

  • Grocery stores, pharmacies — physically have to sell oldest first (expiration dates)
  • Companies that want to show higher profits (to investors, to look healthy)
  • International companies — IFRS (most countries' accounting rules) doesn't allow LIFO

Companies Using LIFO

  • US companies (only US GAAP allows LIFO) — to save taxes when prices rise
  • Industries with rising costs — oil, metals, retail with inflation
  • Companies that don't physically rotate stock — coal piles, gravel, oil tanks

5 Common Mistakes

  1. Mixing up which one is "newest sold." LIFO = Last In, First Out = newest sold first. FIFO = First In, First Out = oldest sold first. Memorize the milk vs. book stack image.
  2. Forgetting Sales Revenue is the SAME under both. Only COGS and Inventory differ. Revenue is whatever you charged customers.
  3. Assuming the physical flow matches the accounting method. A grocery store can use LIFO on paper even if they physically sell oldest milk first. The method is an accounting choice, not a warehouse rule.
  4. Forgetting the "rising prices" assumption matters. If prices are FALLING, the result flips: FIFO would have higher COGS, LIFO lower. The intuition only works when costs are rising.
  5. Forgetting LIFO Reserve. Companies that use LIFO must disclose the difference between LIFO and FIFO inventory in the footnotes. This is called the LIFO Reserve = Inventory at FIFO − Inventory at LIFO.

6 Key Takeaways

  1. FIFO = oldest cost goes to COGS, newest cost stays in Ending Inventory.
  2. LIFO = newest cost goes to COGS, oldest cost stays in Ending Inventory.
  3. When prices rise: FIFO has higher Net Income, LIFO has lower Net Income (and lower tax).
  4. Sales Revenue is the same under both. Only COGS and Inventory differ.
  5. LIFO is only allowed under US GAAP. IFRS bans it.
  6. The accounting method does NOT have to match the physical flow of inventory.

Quick Memory Trick

🥛 FIFO = Fridge (oldest milk first)

📚 LIFO = Last book on stack (newest grabbed first)

FIFO / LIFO — Journal Entries + When to Use Which

Source: Follow-up to Q-007 — "show journal entries and when which case is better" (2026-05-09)

DateActionQtyPriceTotal
Jan 15Purchase10$50$500

Step 1 — Purchase Journal Entries (SAME under both methods)

The purchase entries don't depend on FIFO vs LIFO. You record the actual cash spent and the inventory bought. Method choice only matters when you SELL.

Jan 15 Inventory 500 Cash (or A/P) 500 (To record purchase of 10 units @ $50) Jun 10 Inventory 600 Cash (or A/P) 600 (To record purchase of 10 units @ $60) Nov 20 Inventory 700 Cash (or A/P) 700 (To record purchase of 10 units @ $70)

After all purchases: Inventory T-account has $1,800 ($500 + $600 + $700).

Step 2 — Sale Entries (DIFFERENT under FIFO vs LIFO)

Every sale has two journal entries:

  1. Sales entry — record what the customer paid (revenue + cash). Same under both methods.
  2. COGS entry — move cost of sold goods from Inventory to COGS. Different under FIFO vs LIFO.

(a) Sales Entry — same for both

Dec 5 Cash (or A/R) 2,000 Sales Revenue 2,000 (To record sale of 20 units @ $100)

(b) COGS Entry — Under FIFO

Sold first = oldest. So 10 units from Jan ($50) + 10 units from Jun ($60) = $1,100.

Dec 5 Cost of Goods Sold 1,100 Inventory 1,100 (FIFO: 10×$50 + 10×$60)

Inventory remaining = $700 (the 10 from Nov @ $70).

(b) COGS Entry — Under LIFO

Sold first = newest. So 10 units from Nov ($70) + 10 units from Jun ($60) = $1,300.

Dec 5 Cost of Goods Sold 1,300 Inventory 1,300 (LIFO: 10×$70 + 10×$60)

Inventory remaining = $500 (the 10 from Jan @ $50).

When Is Each Method Better?

✅ FIFO is BETTER when…

  • Prices are FALLING — FIFO gives lower COGS in falling-price environments (because newer cheaper costs stay in inventory, older expensive costs go to COGS first... wait, opposite — when prices fall, oldest cost is HIGHER, so FIFO COGS is HIGHER, NI is LOWER, tax is LOWER. So in falling prices, FIFO actually saves tax!)
  • Showing strong financials matters — investors, lenders, IPO companies want higher reported earnings
  • International (IFRS) companies — IFRS bans LIFO, so FIFO is the only option globally
  • Perishable goods — milk, drugs, food. Even if accounting method is a choice, FIFO matches physical reality
  • Simplicity wanted — FIFO is easier to explain and audit. No LIFO Reserve disclosures needed

✅ LIFO is BETTER when…

  • Prices are RISING (most common case) — LIFO gives higher COGS, lower Net Income, lower TAX. The main reason US companies pick LIFO is the tax savings.
  • Strong cash flow priority — lower tax means more cash retained in the business
  • Industries with persistent inflation — oil, metals, chemicals, retail
  • Stockpile-style inventory — coal piles, gravel, oil tanks where physical newest goes first matches reality
  • US-only operations — only US GAAP allows LIFO. If you'll never need IFRS reporting, LIFO is on the table

The Tax Catch — LIFO Conformity Rule

If you use LIFO for tax, you MUST use LIFO for financial reporting too. The IRS won't let you have it both ways. So companies face a tradeoff:
  • Use LIFO → save tax, but report lower Net Income to investors
  • Use FIFO → higher reported NI to investors, but pay more tax
Most large US companies still pick LIFO because the tax savings outweigh the lower reported earnings.

5 Common Mistakes

  1. Forgetting two entries per sale. Every sale = (1) Cash/AR ↑ Sales Revenue ↑, (2) COGS ↑ Inventory ↓. Both required.
  2. Mixing up "better" intuition. "LIFO saves tax" only when prices are RISING. In falling prices, FIFO saves tax.
  3. Forgetting LIFO Conformity Rule. Can't pick LIFO for tax and FIFO for financials. Pick one for both.
  4. Thinking method affects Sales Revenue. It doesn't. Customer paid the same regardless of which units came off the shelf in your books.
  5. Forgetting LIFO Reserve disclosure. If using LIFO, you must show the difference (Inventory at FIFO − Inventory at LIFO) in the footnotes. Analysts often add it back to get a "FIFO-equivalent" balance sheet.

6 Key Takeaways

  1. Purchase entries are the same under both methods. Sale entries differ only in the COGS amount.
  2. FIFO COGS = oldest cost. LIFO COGS = newest cost. Sales Revenue is identical.
  3. Rising prices → LIFO saves tax. Falling prices → FIFO saves tax. Method matches direction.
  4. LIFO is only allowed under US GAAP. IFRS bans it everywhere else.
  5. LIFO Conformity Rule: if LIFO for tax, then LIFO for financial statements (cannot mix).
  6. The "better" method depends on price direction + tax priority + reporting context. There's no universal winner.

Cash Flow Statement Practice — 5 Trap Problems

Topic: Cash Flow Statement (CFO / CFI / CFF) — Critical concepts + Common mistakes
Instruction: Each problem targets a specific exam trap. Try first, then click "Show Answer" to verify.

💡 The Sign Rule (memorize before starting): Assets and Cash move OPPOSITE. Liabilities and Cash move TOGETHER.

Q1 Working Capital Direction TRAP: sign confusion

For each item, indicate (1) which CFO direction (+ add or − subtract) and (2) why.

  1. Accounts Receivable increased by $30,000 during the year.
  2. Inventory decreased by $20,000.
  3. Accounts Payable decreased by $15,000.
  4. Accrued Liabilities (e.g., wages) increased by $10,000.
  5. Prepaid Insurance increased by $8,000.
▶ Show Answer
ItemDirectionWhy
(a) A/R ↑ $30K− subtractAsset ↑ → Cash ↓. Customers owe more = less cash collected.
(b) Inventory ↓ $20K+ addAsset ↓ → Cash ↑. Used up inventory without buying new = expensed without cash.
(c) A/P ↓ $15K− subtractLiability ↓ → Cash ↓. Paid down vendors = cash out.
(d) Accrued Liab ↑ $10K+ addLiability ↑ → Cash ↑. Owe more wages but didn't pay yet = cash retained.
(e) Prepaid Insurance ↑ $8K− subtractAsset ↑ → Cash ↓. Paid insurance in advance = cash out.
Common Mistake: Reversing the sign for Liabilities. Some students intuitively think "liability went down means I have less debt, so this is good — add". WRONG. Less debt means I PAID it = cash out = subtract.

Q2 Gain on Sale Trap TRAP: double counting

Mountain Co. sold equipment with original cost $200,000 and accumulated depreciation $150,000 for $80,000 cash. Net Income for the year was $500,000.

(a) What is the gain on sale? (b) How does this affect CFO and CFI?

▶ Show Answer

(a) Gain calculation

Book Value = Cost − Accum. Depr = $200,000 − $150,000 = $50,000
Gain = Sale Price − Book Value = $80,000 − $50,000 = $30,000

(b) CFO and CFI treatment

CFO Section:

Net Income $500,000
− Gain on sale ($30,000) ← REMOVE the gain
...other adjustments...

Why subtract the gain? The $30,000 gain is already included in NI. But the actual cash received ($80,000) goes in CFI. If we kept the gain in CFO and put $80,000 in CFI, we'd double-count by $30,000.

CFI Section:

+ Sale of Equipment $80,000 ← FULL CASH (not just the gain)
The trap: Many students put only $30,000 (the gain) in CFI. WRONG. CFI shows the full cash received from sale = $80,000. The gain is removed from CFO so it doesn't appear twice.
Mirror trap: If there had been a LOSS instead, you would ADD the loss back in CFO (because the loss is in NI but real cash is in CFI). Same logic, opposite sign.

Q3 Depreciation with PP&E Sale TRAP: simple ΔA/D rule breaks

Riverside Corp. has the following PP&E and Accumulated Depreciation balances:

BeginningEnding
PP&E (cost)$800,000$1,100,000
Accumulated Depreciation$300,000$340,000

During the year, Riverside sold equipment that originally cost $50,000 (accumulated depreciation on it = $35,000) for $20,000 cash. They also bought new PP&E.

(a) How much was Depreciation Expense for the year? (b) How much PP&E did they purchase?

▶ Show Answer

(a) Depreciation Expense — A/D Rollforward

Beginning A/D + Dep Expense − A/D removed (sold asset) = Ending A/D
$300,000 + X − $35,000 = $340,000
→ X = $340,000 − $300,000 + $35,000 = $75,000

(b) PP&E Purchases — Cost Rollforward

Beginning PP&E + Purchases − Cost of Sold = Ending PP&E
$800,000 + Y − $50,000 = $1,100,000
→ Y = $1,100,000 − $800,000 + $50,000 = $350,000

Sanity check: Book Value of sold asset = $50,000 − $35,000 = $15,000. Sold for $20,000 → gain $5,000 (would appear in NI and need to be subtracted in CFO).

The trap: If you naively compute Depreciation = Ending A/D − Beginning A/D = $40,000, you're WRONG. That ignores the $35,000 A/D that was REMOVED when the equipment was sold. Always do the rollforward when there's a sale.
Same trap for PP&E: Net change ($300K) is NOT the purchase amount because $50K was sold. Purchase = $300K change + $50K sold = $350K.

Q4 Bucket Classification TRAP: which bucket?

Classify each transaction as CFO / CFI / CFF, with sign (+ inflow or − outflow). Use US GAAP rules.

  1. Paid $50,000 cash dividends to shareholders.
  2. Received $30,000 interest income on company's bond investments.
  3. Paid $20,000 interest expense on outstanding bonds.
  4. Issued $500,000 of new common stock for cash.
  5. Bought back $80,000 of treasury stock.
  6. Received $100,000 from sale of investment in another company.
  7. Made a $200,000 long-term loan TO another company.
  8. Paid $30,000 to retire bonds at maturity.
▶ Show Answer
ItemBucketSignNote
(a) Dividends paidCFF− $50KAlways CFF. Never on Income Statement.
(b) Interest income receivedCFO+ $30KUS GAAP: CFO. (IFRS allows CFI choice.)
(c) Interest expense paidCFO− $20KUS GAAP: CFO. (IFRS allows CFF choice.)
(d) Issued common stockCFF+ $500KCash from owners.
(e) Bought treasury stockCFF− $80KCash to owners (reverse).
(f) Sold investment in another co.CFI+ $100KInvestment activity.
(g) Made loan to another co.CFI− $200KLending IS investing.
(h) Retired bondsCFF− $30KRepayment of principal.
Trap 1: Putting dividends in CFO. Dividends are NEVER on the Income Statement and ALWAYS in CFF. They reduce Retained Earnings directly.
Trap 2: Putting interest paid in CFF (because it relates to debt). Under US GAAP, interest paid is in CFO. Under IFRS, it's a choice. Know which framework you're under.
Trap 3: Confusing loan issuance vs loan to others. Issuing OWN bonds = CFF (you're financing yourself). Lending TO others = CFI (you're investing).

Q5 Full CFS Reconciliation TRAP: integrating everything

BlueGrass Inc. provided the following information for 2026:

  • Net Income: $400,000
  • Depreciation Expense: $90,000
  • Loss on sale of equipment: $5,000 (sold equipment with book value $25,000 for $20,000 cash)
  • A/R increased by $40,000
  • Inventory increased by $25,000
  • A/P decreased by $15,000
  • Issued bonds for $200,000 cash
  • Purchased PP&E for $350,000 cash
  • Paid dividends of $80,000
  • Beginning Cash: $50,000

Compute Net CFO, Net CFI, Net CFF, and Ending Cash.

▶ Show Answer

CFO

Net Income400,000
+ Depreciation (non-cash)+90,000
+ Loss on sale (add back, real cash in CFI)+5,000
− A/R increase (asset ↑ → cash ↓)−40,000
− Inventory increase (asset ↑ → cash ↓)−25,000
− A/P decrease (liability ↓ → cash ↓)−15,000
Net CFO$415,000

CFI

+ Sale of equipment (full cash)+20,000
− Purchase of PP&E−350,000
Net CFI($330,000)

CFF

+ Issued bonds+200,000
− Dividends paid−80,000
Net CFF$120,000

Reconciliation

Net Change in Cash = $415,000 + ($330,000) + $120,000 = $205,000
Beginning Cash $50,000 + $205,000 = Ending Cash $255,000
Trap 1: Forgetting to ADD the loss back. Loss of $5,000 reduced NI but no cash actually left through the income statement (the cash impact is the actual sale price). Always reverse non-cash gains/losses in CFO.
Trap 2: Putting the gain or loss (not the full cash) in CFI. You put $20,000 in CFI (full sale price), not $5,000 (the loss).
Trap 3: Forgetting any item. Five items (or more) under each section is normal. Build a checklist habit: NI → non-cash → working capital → CFI assets → CFF debt + equity + dividends.

Top 5 Cash Flow Statement Traps (Memorize)

  1. Sign confusion in working capital. Assets and Cash move OPPOSITE. Liabilities and Cash move TOGETHER.
  2. Double-counting on asset sales. Remove the gain (or add back the loss) in CFO. Put the FULL cash sale price in CFI, not the gain.
  3. Naive depreciation calculation when a sale exists. The simple "Dep = ΔA/D" breaks. Always do the A/D rollforward (BB + Dep − A/D removed = EB).
  4. Wrong bucket for dividends, interest, loans to others. Dividends always CFF. Interest paid CFO under US GAAP. Lending TO others CFI (not CFF).
  5. Missing a reconciliation step. Always end with: Begin Cash + (CFO + CFI + CFF) = End Cash. If it doesn't tie, hunt the error.

6 Notes You MUST Know

  1. The 3 buckets must reconcile to the change in Cash (Begin Cash + Net Cash Flow = End Cash).
  2. Indirect Method for CFO is the most common — start with NI, adjust for non-cash and working capital.
  3. Non-cash items added back: Depreciation, Amortization, Impairment, Stock-based comp, Loss on sale.
  4. Non-cash items subtracted: Gain on sale (real cash is in CFI).
  5. Dividends, share buybacks, debt issuance, debt repayment all go to CFF.
  6. Working capital sign rules: Asset ↑ = Cash ↓ (subtract). Liability ↑ = Cash ↑ (add). Mirror logic.

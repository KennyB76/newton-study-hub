Cash Flow Statement — Calculation Deep Dive

Source: Follow-up to Q-005 — "more help on cash flow calculation with CFO CFI CFF" (2026-05-09)

The Master Equation

Beginning Cash + (CFO + CFI + CFF) = Ending Cash

Everything else is just figuring out which bucket each transaction goes into, and the direction (inflow + or outflow −).

Step-by-Step Recipe (Indirect Method)

1 Start with Net Income (top of CFO section).
This is your starting cash proxy.
2 Add back non-cash expenses.
These reduced NI but didn't actually take cash out.
3 Subtract non-cash gains, add non-cash losses.
These are in NI but the actual cash is in CFI (sale of asset).
4 Adjust for working capital changes.
Match the sign rules below.
5 That's your CFO. Then do CFI (asset transactions) and CFF (debt + equity transactions) separately.

The Sign Rules — Memorize These

For CFO Working Capital Adjustments

Asset goes UP → Cash goes DOWN (subtract)
Example: A/R ↑ $50K means customers owe you more. You earned the revenue (in NI) but didn't collect cash. So subtract $50K.
Asset goes DOWN → Cash goes UP (add)
Example: Inventory ↓ $30K means you used inventory without buying new. You expensed it (in NI) but didn't pay cash. So add $30K.
Liability goes UP → Cash goes UP (add)
Example: A/P ↑ $20K means you owe vendors more. You expensed something (in NI) but didn't pay cash. So add $20K.
Liability goes DOWN → Cash goes DOWN (subtract)
Example: Taxes Payable ↓ $10K means you paid down taxes. You'd already expensed them (in NI) earlier. So subtract $10K.

Mnemonic

"Assets and Cash move OPPOSITE. Liabilities and Cash move TOGETHER."

Non-Cash Items to Adjust in CFO

ItemDirectionWhy
Depreciation+ (add back)Reduced NI but no cash paid
Amortization+ (add back)Same as depreciation, intangibles
Impairment+ (add back)Write-down of asset, no cash
Stock-based compensation+ (add back)Paid in stock, not cash
Gain on sale of asset− (subtract)Real cash from sale is in CFI, not CFO
Loss on sale of asset+ (add back)Reduced NI but real cash is in CFI
Bad debt expense+ (add back, sometimes)Non-cash unless written off

What Goes Where — The 3 Buckets

CFO — Cash Flow from Operations

  • Net Income (starting point)
  • Depreciation, Amortization, Impairment (add back)
  • Gains/Losses on sale (remove from NI)
  • Changes in working capital: A/R, Inventory, A/P, Accrued Liabilities, Prepaid Expenses, Taxes Payable, Unearned Revenue

CFI — Cash Flow from Investing

  • Purchase of PP&E (− cash out)
  • Sale of PP&E (+ cash in, at actual sale price, not book value)
  • Purchase of Land, Buildings, Equipment
  • Purchase / sale of investments (stocks, bonds of other companies)
  • Loans made to others / collected from others

CFF — Cash Flow from Financing

  • Issuance of stock (+ cash in)
  • Buy-back of stock (− cash out)
  • Issuance of bonds / loans (+ cash in)
  • Repayment of bonds / loans principal (− cash out)
  • Dividends paid (− cash out)
  • APIC contributions
Gotcha — Interest paid?
Under US GAAP: Interest paid is in CFO (operations).
Under IFRS: Interest paid can be in CFO or CFF (company's choice).
Same for interest received and dividends received.

The 4 "Special Calculations" Always Tested

① Dividend Paid (from RE rollforward)

Beginning RE + Net Income − Dividends = Ending RE
Solve for Dividends.

② Depreciation Expense (when there's a sale)

Beginning A/D + Depreciation Expense − A/D removed (for sold asset) = Ending A/D
Solve for Depreciation Expense.
(A/D removed = Original Cost of sold asset − Book Value at sale)

③ PP&E Purchases (from Cost rollforward)

Beginning PP&E (Cost) + Purchases − Cost of sold asset = Ending PP&E (Cost)
Solve for Purchases.

④ Cash Received from PP&E Sale

Cash received = Book Value at sale + Gain (or − Loss)
This goes in CFI as a positive cash inflow.

5 Common Mistakes

  1. Putting interest paid in CFF. Under US GAAP, it's in CFO. Common mistake under IFRS rules.
  2. Putting only the gain (not the cash sale price) in CFI. CFI shows the FULL cash received from sale, not just the gain. The gain is removed from NI in CFO.
  3. Forgetting depreciation when there's a sale. A/D rollforward is mandatory — the simple ΔA/D rule breaks.
  4. Wrong sign on working capital. Memorize: Assets opposite of cash. Liabilities same as cash.
  5. Treating dividends as expense. Dividends are NOT on the Income Statement. They reduce Retained Earnings directly. They show up only in CFF and the RE rollforward.

6 Key Takeaways

  1. 3 buckets: CFO (operations), CFI (assets), CFF (debt + equity). They must reconcile to the change in Cash.
  2. Indirect method = NI → add back non-cash → adjust for working capital → CFO.
  3. Sign rule: Assets opposite of cash. Liabilities same as cash.
  4. When PP&E is sold: do BOTH rollforwards (Cost AND A/D). Sale price (full cash) goes in CFI; gain/loss removed from CFO.
  5. Dividends are in CFF only (not Income Statement). Use RE rollforward to find amount.
  6. Sanity check always: Beginning Cash + (CFO + CFI + CFF) = Ending Cash. If it doesn't tie, find the error.

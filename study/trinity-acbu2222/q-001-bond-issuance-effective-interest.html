Bond Issuance — Effective Interest Method

Newton Corp · Premium amortization · 5 parts · 2026-05-08

Question

📝 Given
Newton Corp issues 4-year, $100,000 face value bonds on January 1, 2026. Stated rate = 6% (annual) Interest paid annually on December 31 Market rate at issuance = 5% Issue price = $103,545.95
🎯 Required (5 parts)
  1. (a) Journal entry on January 1, 2026 (issue date).
  2. (b) Bond amortization schedule (4 years, effective interest method).
  3. (c) Journal entry on December 31, 2026 (year 1 interest payment + premium amortization).
  4. (d) Carrying value of bonds on December 31, 2027 (end of year 2).
  5. (e) Total interest expense recognized over the 4-year life of the bond.

Setup — Key Formulas & Numbers

① Cash Interest (constant every year)
Cash Interest = Face × Stated Rate = $100,000 × 6% = $6,000
② Interest Expense (effective interest method)
Interest Expense = Beginning Carrying Value × Market Rate (5%)
③ Premium Amortization
Premium Amort = Cash Interest − Interest Expense
④ Ending Carrying Value
Ending CV = Beginning CV − Premium Amortization

Total premium at issue = $103,545.95 − $100,000 = $3,545.95. This will fully amortize to $0 over 4 years (CV returns to face value at maturity).

(a) Issue Date — January 1, 2026
Receive cash, recognize face value of bonds and premium separately.
Cash103,545.95
Bonds Payable100,000.00
Premium on Bonds Payable3,545.95

Why split? Bonds Payable always records at face value. The extra cash received above face becomes Premium on Bonds Payable (a liability adjunct account).

(b) Amortization Schedule (4 years)
Each year: Interest Expense = prior CV × 5%. Premium amortization = $6,000 − Interest Expense. CV decreases until it reaches face value $100,000.

(c) Year 1 Entry — December 31, 2026
Pay $6,000 cash. Recognize $5,177.30 interest expense. Amortize $822.70 of premium (debit, because premium decreases).
Interest Expense5,177.30
Premium on Bonds Payable822.70
Cash6,000.00

Why is Premium debited? Premium has a credit balance (it adds to Bonds Payable to form CV). When CV decreases, Premium decreases — and decreasing a credit balance means we debit it.

(d) Carrying Value on December 31, 2027
End of year 2 = "End CV" column in row 2 of the schedule.
📌 Answer
Carrying Value (12/31/2027) = $101,859.41

Breakdown: Bonds Payable $100,000 + Premium on Bonds Payable $1,859.41 = $101,859.41.

(e) Total Interest Expense — 4 Years
Sum of the Interest Expense column. Or use the shortcut: Total Cash Interest − Total Premium Amortized.
Method 1 — sum the schedule
5,177.30 + 5,136.16 + 5,092.97 + 5,047.62 = $20,454.05
Method 2 — shortcut
(Cash Interest × 4) − Total Premium = ($6,000 × 4) − $3,545.95
= $24,000 − $3,545.95 = $20,454.05
📌 Answer
Total Interest Expense (4 years) = $20,454.05

Intuition: Even though we pay out $24,000 in cash interest, our actual borrowing cost is only $20,454.05 — because we initially received an extra $3,545.95 (the premium) above what we owe at maturity.

Common Mistakes (must-avoid traps)

⚠ The 5 traps that cost points on this exact question type
  • Stated rate vs Market rate confusion
    Cash Interest uses the stated rate × face. Interest Expense uses the market rate × CV. Mixing these = wrong on every line.
  • Premium vs Discount direction
    Issue price > face → premium → Int Exp < Cash Int → premium amortizes down. Issue price < face → discount → Int Exp > Cash Int → discount amortizes up. They move in opposite directions.
  • Wrong sign on Premium in the journal entry
    When amortizing, premium is debited (because credit balance is decreasing). Many students credit it by accident.
  • CV update timing
    Use beginning CV (not ending) to compute that year's Interest Expense. Year N's ending CV becomes Year N+1's beginning CV.
  • Carrying value composition
    CV = Bonds Payable (always face) + Unamortized Premium (or − Unamortized Discount). Don't confuse CV with Bonds Payable alone.
💡 Key Takeaway — 30-second review before the exam
  • Interest Expense = CV × market rate. That's the whole effective interest method in one line.
  • Cash Interest is constant = face × stated rate. The volatility is in Interest Expense, not Cash.
  • Premium: CV moves down toward face. Interest Expense decreases each year.
  • Discount: CV moves up toward face. Interest Expense increases each year.
  • Total Interest Expense (life of bond) = Total Cash Interest − Premium (or + Discount). Always.
  • At maturity, CV always equals face value. Premium/Discount has fully amortized to zero.

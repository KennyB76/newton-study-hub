Q1 Basic FIFO vs LIFO COGS Calculation (Rising Prices)

Bluebird Co. had the following inventory transactions:

DateActionQtyUnit Cost
Jan 1Beginning Inventory100$8
Mar 5Purchase200$10
Aug 12Purchase150$12
Dec 31Sold during the year300—

Compute COGS and Ending Inventory under (a) FIFO, (b) LIFO, (c) Weighted Average.

▶ Show Answer

Total units available = 100 + 200 + 150 = 450. Sold 300, so ending units = 150.

Total cost available = (100×$8) + (200×$10) + (150×$12) = $800 + $2,000 + $1,800 = $4,600.

(a) FIFO — oldest goes to COGS

Sold first = 100 @ $8 + 200 @ $10 = $800 + $2,000 = COGS $2,800
Ending Inv = 150 left @ $12 = $1,800

(b) LIFO — newest goes to COGS

Sold first = 150 @ $12 + 150 @ $10 = $1,800 + $1,500 = COGS $3,300
Ending Inv = 50 left @ $10 + 100 @ $8 = $500 + $800 = $1,300

(c) Weighted Average

Avg cost = $4,600 / 450 = $10.222 per unit
COGS = 300 × $10.222 = $3,067 (rounded)
Ending Inv = 150 × $10.222 = $1,533 (rounded)
Common Mistake: Forgetting Beginning Inventory! Many students treat the year as if only purchases existed. Always include BB Inv in available units and cost.

Q2 Journal Entries — Both FIFO and LIFO

Skyline Corp made the following 2 transactions:

  1. Mar 1: Bought 50 widgets at $20 each on account.
  2. Mar 15: Sold 30 widgets for $50 each, cash. Beginning Inventory was 20 widgets at $15 each.

Show ALL journal entries on Mar 15 under (a) FIFO and (b) LIFO.

▶ Show Answer

Mar 1 — Purchase entry (same under both)

Mar 1 Inventory 1,000 Accounts Payable 1,000 (50 widgets × $20)

Mar 15 — Sales entry (same under both)

Mar 15 Cash 1,500 Sales Revenue 1,500 (30 widgets × $50)

Mar 15 — COGS entry under FIFO

Sold first = 20 @ $15 (BB) + 10 @ $20 (Mar 1) = $300 + $200 = $500

Mar 15 Cost of Goods Sold 500 Inventory 500 (FIFO: 20 × $15 + 10 × $20)

Mar 15 — COGS entry under LIFO

Sold first = 30 @ $20 (Mar 1, all newest) = $600

Mar 15 Cost of Goods Sold 600 Inventory 600 (LIFO: 30 × $20)
Common Mistake: Forgetting that EVERY sale needs TWO entries — (1) Cash/AR + Sales Revenue, (2) COGS + Inventory. Many students only write one.

Q3 Falling Prices — Which Method Saves Tax?

TechWidget Inc. (US-based) experienced FALLING prices in the computer chip market this year:

DateActionQtyUnit Cost
JanPurchase100$50
JulPurchase100$40
NovPurchase100$30

Sold 200 units at $80 each. Tax rate 25%.

Compute COGS, NI, and Tax under FIFO and LIFO. Which method saves tax in falling prices?

▶ Show Answer

Sales Revenue = 200 × $80 = $16,000.

FIFO (oldest, $50 + $40)

COGS = 100 × $50 + 100 × $40 = $9,000
Gross Profit = $16,000 − $9,000 = $7,000
Tax = $7,000 × 25% = $1,750

LIFO (newest, $30 + $40)

COGS = 100 × $30 + 100 × $40 = $7,000
Gross Profit = $16,000 − $7,000 = $9,000
Tax = $9,000 × 25% = $2,250

Conclusion: In FALLING prices, FIFO has higher COGS → lower NI → FIFO saves tax. The intuition flips from rising-price scenarios.

Common Mistake (this is the trap): Memorizing "LIFO saves tax" without the price-direction qualifier. LIFO only saves tax when prices RISE. In falling prices, FIFO is better for tax. Tech and consumer electronics companies often face deflation and pick FIFO accordingly.

Q4 Moving Average (Perpetual)

HappyMart uses Perpetual Moving Average. Transactions in chronological order:

DateActionQtyUnit Cost
Apr 1Beginning Inv10$50
Apr 5Purchase10$60
Apr 10Sale8—
Apr 15Purchase10$70
Apr 20Sale10—

Compute (a) the average cost AT EACH POINT, (b) total COGS for April, and (c) Ending Inventory $.

▶ Show Answer

(a) Average cost at each point

After Apr 1 (BB): 10 units × $50 = $500. Avg = $50.
After Apr 5 (purchase): 10 + 10 = 20 units. Cost = $500 + $600 = $1,100. Avg = $55.
After Apr 10 (sold 8): 12 units left. Cost = 12 × $55 = $660. Avg still $55.
After Apr 15 (purchase): 12 + 10 = 22 units. Cost = $660 + $700 = $1,360. Avg = $61.82.
After Apr 20 (sold 10): 12 units left. Avg still $61.82.

(b) Total COGS

Apr 10 sale: 8 × $55 = $440
Apr 20 sale: 10 × $61.82 = $618.20
Total COGS = $1,058.20

(c) Ending Inventory

12 units × $61.82 = $741.80

Sanity check: BB + Purchases − COGS = End Inv → $500 + $600 + $700 − $1,058.20 = $741.80 ✓

Common Mistake: Recomputing the average AFTER A SALE. Sales don't change the average — only purchases do. The average gets re-set when new units come IN.

Q5 Method Choice — Which One Should Each Company Pick?

Match each company below with its best inventory method (FIFO / LIFO / Average / Specific ID). Justify each in 1 sentence.

  1. Kroger — US grocery chain, perishable food, prices generally rising.
  2. Tesla Energy — global lithium battery manufacturer reporting under IFRS, prices have been falling steadily.
  3. ExxonMobil — US oil major, commingled crude oil tanks, persistent inflation in commodity prices.
  4. Sotheby's Auction House — sells unique paintings and vintage cars, each item priced individually.
  5. SK Hynix (Korea) — semiconductor memory, IFRS reporter, prices fluctuate widely month to month.
▶ Show Answer
  1. Kroger → FIFO. Perishable food MUST physically rotate (oldest milk first), so FIFO matches reality. Even though LIFO would save tax in rising prices, food spoilage and customer-safety regulations dominate the choice.
  2. Tesla Energy → FIFO (or Average). IFRS bans LIFO. Falling prices mean FIFO has higher COGS and thus saves tax (the opposite of rising prices intuition). Average is also fine and smooths volatility.
  3. ExxonMobil → LIFO. US GAAP allows LIFO. Rising commodity prices mean LIFO gives higher COGS, lower NI, lower tax — major savings. Crude oil is also commingled, so the physical flow argument doesn't restrict choice.
  4. Sotheby's → Specific Identification. Each item is unique and high-value. You can (and must) track each item's actual cost. FIFO/LIFO/Average wouldn't make sense for one-of-a-kind goods.
  5. SK Hynix → Average (Weighted). IFRS bans LIFO. Volatile prices mean Average smooths COGS swings month-to-month, giving steadier reported earnings. Also matches the commingled nature of memory chip inventory.
Common Mistake: Picking LIFO for an IFRS company. IFRS bans LIFO globally. If a company reports under IFRS (most non-US public companies), the only choices are FIFO, Average, or Specific ID.

5 Things to Watch For (Common Mistakes)

  1. Forgetting Beginning Inventory in available units and cost.
  2. Writing only the Sales entry without the COGS entry. Each sale = 2 journal entries.
  3. Memorizing "LIFO saves tax" without the rising-price condition. In falling prices, FIFO saves tax.
  4. Recomputing the average after a sale in Moving Average. The average only changes when new units come IN.
  5. Using LIFO for an IFRS company. LIFO is only allowed under US GAAP.

6 Key Takeaways

  1. Total Cost Available = Beginning Inv + Purchases. Total Units Available = same logic.
  2. FIFO COGS = oldest. LIFO COGS = newest. Average = blended.
  3. Sales Revenue is identical across all methods — only COGS and Ending Inventory differ.
  4. Rising prices: FIFO higher NI, LIFO lower NI (tax saving). Falling prices: opposite.
  5. Two variants of Average: Periodic (calc once at end) vs Perpetual (recalc each purchase).
  6. Method must match the regulatory environment: US GAAP allows all 4. IFRS bans LIFO.

Part 1 — FIFO / LIFO Journal Entries + When to Use Which

Setup — Skateboard Example

DateActionQtyPriceTotal
Jan 15Purchase10$50$500
Jun 10Purchase10$60$600
Nov 20Purchase10$70$700
Dec 5Sale20$100$2,000

Step 1 — Purchase Entries (SAME under FIFO and LIFO)

Method choice only matters when you SELL. Purchases are recorded the same way:

Jan 15 Inventory 500 Cash (or A/P) 500 Jun 10 Inventory 600 Cash (or A/P) 600 Nov 20 Inventory 700 Cash (or A/P) 700

Total Inventory before sale: $1,800

Step 2 — Sale Entries (DIFFER under FIFO vs LIFO)

Every sale needs two entries:

(a) Sales Entry — Same for Both

Dec 5 Cash (or A/R) 2,000 Sales Revenue 2,000

(b) COGS Entry — Under FIFO (oldest first)

Dec 5 Cost of Goods Sold 1,100 Inventory 1,100 (10 from Jan @ $50 + 10 from Jun @ $60)

Inventory remaining: $700 (the 10 from Nov @ $70)

(b) COGS Entry — Under LIFO (newest first)

Dec 5 Cost of Goods Sold 1,300 Inventory 1,300 (10 from Nov @ $70 + 10 from Jun @ $60)

Inventory remaining: $500 (the 10 from Jan @ $50)

Side-by-Side Impact

FIFOLIFODifference
Sales Revenue$2,000$2,0000 (same)
COGS$1,100$1,300$200 higher under LIFO
Gross Profit$900$700$200 lower under LIFO
Tax (assume 25%)$225$175$50 lower under LIFO
Net Income$675$525$150 lower under LIFO
Ending Inventory (BS)$700$500$200 lower under LIFO

When Each Is Better

✅ FIFO is BETTER when…

  • Prices are FALLING — FIFO COGS uses oldest (higher) costs → higher COGS → lower NI → tax saving
  • Showing strong financials matters (investors, IPO, lenders)
  • International (IFRS) companies — IFRS bans LIFO globally
  • Perishable goods — milk, drugs, food (FIFO matches physical reality)
  • Simplicity — easier to audit, no LIFO Reserve disclosure

✅ LIFO is BETTER when…

  • Prices are RISING (most common) — LIFO COGS uses newest (higher) costs → higher COGS → lower NI → lower tax
  • Cash flow priority — tax savings retain cash in business
  • Industries with persistent inflation — oil, metals, retail
  • Stockpile inventory — coal, gravel, oil tanks (newest physically goes first)
  • US-only operations — only US GAAP allows LIFO

The Tax Catch — LIFO Conformity Rule

If LIFO for tax, you MUST use LIFO for financial reporting too. The IRS won't let you mix.
  • LIFO → save tax, but report lower NI to investors
  • FIFO → higher reported NI, but pay more tax

Part 1b — Average Cost Method (Weighted Average)

The third inventory cost flow method — sits between FIFO and LIFO.

The Formula

Weighted Average Cost per Unit = Total Cost of Goods Available ÷ Total Units Available

Then BOTH COGS and Ending Inventory use this same average.

Same Skateboard Example — Average Cost

Total Cost = $500 + $600 + $700 = $1,800
Total Units = 10 + 10 + 10 = 30
Weighted Average = $1,800 / 30 = $60 per unit

Sold 20 units, 10 left over. Both use $60:

COGS = 20 × $60 = $1,200
Ending Inventory = 10 × $60 = $600

Journal Entries — Average Cost

Purchases: same as FIFO/LIFO. Sales entry (cash) also same. Only the COGS entry uses the average:

Dec 5 Cost of Goods Sold 1,200 Inventory 1,200 (Average: 20 × $60)

3-Way Comparison

FIFOAverageLIFO
Sales Revenue$2,000$2,000$2,000
COGS$1,100$1,200$1,300
Gross Profit$900$800$700
Tax (25%)$225$200$175
Net Income$675$600$525
Ending Inventory$700$600$500
Pattern: When prices RISE, results sit in this order:
  • Highest NI / lowest COGS / highest End Inv → FIFO
  • Middle ground → Average
  • Lowest NI / highest COGS / lowest End Inv → LIFO
In FALLING prices, the order flips.

When Average Cost is Better

  • Mixed/blended inventory — gasoline, grain, oil, chemicals where individual units are physically indistinguishable. You can't tell which unit was purchased when.
  • Volatile prices — Average smooths out wild swings. Less noise in COGS month-to-month.
  • Administrative simplicity — one calculation per period, no need to track each layer.
  • International (IFRS) compliance — both GAAP and IFRS allow Average. (LIFO is banned in IFRS, so multinationals often pick Average over FIFO for global consistency.)
  • Industries with frequent re-pricing — Apple's logistics, Amazon, retailers using continuous flow.

2 Variants of Average

Periodic (Weighted Average)

Calculate one average for the whole period at the end. Multiply by units sold and units left.

Formula: Total Cost ÷ Total Units (we used this above).

Perpetual (Moving Average)

Recalculate the average every time you buy more inventory. So during the year, the cost per unit changes after each purchase.

After Jan: avg = $50
After Jun (10 more @ $60): avg = ($500 + $600) / 20 = $55
After Nov (10 more @ $70): avg = ($1,100 + $700) / 30 = $60
Each sale uses the average AT THAT MOMENT.
Gotcha: Moving Average gives slightly different COGS than Weighted Average if you have sales between purchases. Same example, different numbers depending on when sales hit.

Specific Identification — The 4th Method (FYI)

For unique high-value items (cars, fine art, jewelry, custom equipment), companies can track each individual unit's actual cost. COGS = exact cost of the specific unit sold. Not common in commodity businesses but worth knowing.

Part 2 — Cash Flow Calculation Deep Dive

The Master Equation

Beginning Cash + (CFO + CFI + CFF) = Ending Cash

Everything is just which bucket each transaction goes into + direction (inflow + or outflow −).

Step-by-Step Recipe (Indirect Method)

1 Start with Net Income (top of CFO).
2 Add back non-cash expenses (Depreciation, Amortization, Impairment, Stock-based comp).
3 Subtract gains, add losses (real cash from asset sales is in CFI, not CFO).
4 Adjust for working capital changes (sign rules below).
5 That's CFO. Then do CFI (assets) and CFF (debt + equity) separately.

The Sign Rules — Memorize

Asset UP → Cash DOWN (subtract) — A/R ↑ means customers owe more = less cash collected
Asset DOWN → Cash UP (add) — Inventory ↓ means used inventory without buying new = expensed without cash out
Liability UP → Cash UP (add) — A/P ↑ means owe vendors more = expensed without cash out
Liability DOWN → Cash DOWN (subtract) — Taxes Payable ↓ means paid tax = cash out

Mnemonic: "Assets and Cash move OPPOSITE. Liabilities and Cash move TOGETHER."

Non-Cash Items in CFO

ItemDirectionWhy
Depreciation+ add backReduced NI but no cash paid
Amortization+ add backSame as depreciation, intangibles
Impairment+ add backWrite-down, no cash
Stock-based compensation+ add backPaid in stock, not cash
Gain on sale of asset− subtractReal cash from sale is in CFI

The 3 Buckets — What Goes Where

CFO — Operations

  • Net Income (start)
  • Non-cash: Depreciation, Amortization, Impairment, SBC
  • Gains/losses on asset sales (remove)
  • Working capital changes: A/R, Inventory, A/P, Accrued, Prepaid, Taxes Payable, Unearned Revenue

CFI — Investing

  • Purchase of PP&E (− out)
  • Sale of PP&E (+ in, at actual sale price, not book value)
  • Purchase of Land, Buildings, Investments
  • Loans made / collected

CFF — Financing

  • Issuance of stock (+ in)
  • Buy-back of stock (− out)
  • Issuance of bonds / loans (+ in)
  • Repayment of principal (− out)
  • Dividends paid (− out)
  • APIC contributions
Interest paid? US GAAP: in CFO. IFRS: choice (CFO or CFF).

4 Special Calculations Always Tested

① Dividend Paid (RE rollforward)

Beginning RE + NI − Dividends = Ending RE

② Depreciation Expense (when there's a sale)

Beginning A/D + Dep Expense − A/D removed = Ending A/D
(A/D removed = Cost − Book Value of sold asset)

③ PP&E Purchases

Beginning PP&E (cost) + Purchases − Cost of sold = Ending PP&E

④ Cash Received from Sale

Cash = Book Value + Gain (or − Loss)
Goes in CFI as full cash inflow.

Combined Key Takeaways (Both Topics)

Inventory Cost Flow Methods (FIFO / LIFO / Average)

  1. Purchase entries are same under all 3. Sale entries differ only in COGS amount.
  2. FIFO COGS = oldest cost. LIFO COGS = newest cost. Average COGS = blended unit cost.
  3. Sales Revenue is identical under all 3 methods.
  4. Order in rising prices: FIFO highest NI → Average middle → LIFO lowest NI (and tax).
  5. LIFO only allowed under US GAAP. IFRS allows FIFO and Average only.
  6. LIFO Conformity: if LIFO for tax, then LIFO for financials (cannot mix).
  7. Average has 2 sub-variants: Periodic (Weighted Avg, end-of-period) vs Perpetual (Moving Avg, recalc each purchase).

Cash Flow Statement

  1. 3 buckets: CFO (operations), CFI (assets), CFF (debt + equity).
  2. Indirect method: NI → add non-cash → adjust working capital → CFO.
  3. Sign rule: Assets opposite of cash. Liabilities same as cash.
  4. When PP&E is sold: BOTH rollforwards (Cost AND A/D). Full sale cash in CFI; gain removed from CFO.
  5. Dividends only in CFF (never on Income Statement). Use RE rollforward.
  6. Sanity check: Begin Cash + (CFO + CFI + CFF) = End Cash.

Memory Tricks

  • 🥛 FIFO = Milk in fridge (oldest first)
  • 📚 LIFO = Stack of books (newest grabbed first)
  • ↔️ Assets and Cash = OPPOSITE direction in CFO
  • ↕️ Liabilities and Cash = SAME direction in CFO

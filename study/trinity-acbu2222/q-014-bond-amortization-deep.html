PART 1 — Concept: Why Bonds Are Issued at a Discount or Premium

Three Scenarios at Issuance

ScenarioRelationshipIssue PriceAmortization
Par Stated Rate = Market Rate = Face Value None (no premium, no discount)
Discount Stated Rate < Market Rate < Face Value (bond is cheap) Carrying Value walks UP to Face
Premium Stated Rate > Market Rate > Face Value (bond is expensive) Carrying Value walks DOWN to Face
Intuition: Market rate = what investors DEMAND. Stated rate = what the bond PAYS. If the bond pays less than the market demands → investors pay less (discount). If it pays more → investors pay more (premium).

PART 3 — Worked Example (Discount Bond, Full Amortization Table + Journal Entries)

Given Information

  • Face = $100,000  |  Term = 5 years  |  Stated Rate = 6% annual  |  Market Rate = 8% annual
  • Interest paid semiannually → 10 periods, period rate: Stated = 3%, Market = 4%

Step 1 — Compute Issue Price (PV Calculation)

PV of Face = 100,000 × (1.04)^−10 = 100,000 × 0.67556 = $67,556 PV of Annuity = 3,000 × [(1 − (1.04)^−10) / 0.04] = 3,000 × [0.32444 / 0.04] = 3,000 × 8.1109 = $24,333 Issue Price = 67,556 + 24,333 = $91,889 (rounded to nearest dollar)
Discount = $100,000 − $91,889 = $8,111. CV starts below Face → walks UP.

Problem 1 Discount Bond — Full Cycle

SolarTech Corp. issues bonds on Jan 1, Year 1:

(a) Compute the issue price.

▶ Show Answer (a)

Problem 2 Premium Bond + Early Retirement

LakeFront Industries issues bonds on Jan 1, Year 1:

  • Face = $200,000  |  Term = 8 years  |  Stated Rate = 8%  |  Market Rate = 6%  |  Annual interest
  • 20 annual periods? No — annual, so 8 periods total.

(a) Compute the issue price.

▶ Show Answer (a)
PV of Face = 200,000 × (1.06)^−8 = 200,000 × 0.62741 = $125,482 PV of Annuity = 16,000 × [(1 − (1.06)^−8) / 0.06] = 16,000 × [0.37259 / 0.06] = 16,000 × 6.2098 = $99,357 Issue Price = 125,482 + 99,357 = $224,839 Premium = 224,839 − 200,000 = $24,839
Stated 8% > Market 6% → premium. Issue price > face. CV will walk DOWN over 8 years.

▶ Show Answer (d)

Debit the face + remaining premium (together = CV). Credit cash for what you actually pay. Difference = Gain or Loss.

Problem 3 Comparison Problem (Discount vs Premium)

Two bonds issued same date, same face, same term:

  • Bond A: Face $100,000  |  5-year  |  Stated 4%  |  Market 6%  |  Annual interest
  • Bond B: Face $100,000  |  5-year  |  Stated 6%  |  Market 4%  |  Annual interest

(a) Which is discount, which is premium? Why?

▶ Show Answer (a)
  • Bond A = DISCOUNT. Stated 4% < Market 6%. Investors demand more than the bond pays → they pay less than face.
  • Bond B = PREMIUM. Stated 6% > Market 4%. Bond pays more than investors demand → they pay more than face.

(b) Compute issue price for each.

▶ Show Answer (b) Bond A (Discount, Market = 6%):
PV of Face = 100,000 × (1.06)^−5 = 100,000 × 0.74726 = $74,726 PV of Annuity = 4,000 × [(1 − (1.06)^−5) / 0.06] = 4,000 × [0.25274 / 0.06] = 4,000 × 4.21236 = $16,849 Issue Price A = 74,726 + 16,849 = $91,575 → Discount = $8,425
Bond B (Premium, Market = 4%):
PV of Face = 100,000 × (1.04)^−5 = 100,000 × 0.82193 = $82,193 PV of Annuity = 6,000 × [(1 − (1.04)^−5) / 0.04] = 6,000 × [0.17807 / 0.04] = 6,000 × 4.45182 = $26,711 Issue Price B = 82,193 + 26,711 = $108,904 → Premium = $8,904

(d) After Year 1, which bond has higher Interest Expense? Why?

▶ Show Answer (d)
  • Bond A Interest Expense (Year 1) = $5,495
  • Bond B Interest Expense (Year 1) = $4,356
  • Bond A is higher.
Why? Bond A has a higher market rate (6% vs 4%). Interest Expense = CV × market rate. Even though Bond A's CV ($91,575) < Bond B's CV ($108,904), the rate effect dominates: 91,575 × 6% = 5,495 > 108,904 × 4% = 4,356.

(e) Carrying Value of each at maturity? Total Interest Expense over life of each?

▶ Show Answer (e)

CV at maturity: Both bonds = $100,000 exactly. That is always the case by definition of amortization.

Total Interest Expense over 5 years:

Bond A (Discount): Total cash paid = 5 × $4,000 = $20,000 (coupon) + $100,000 (face at maturity) = $120,000 Total received at issuance = $91,575 Total Interest Expense = 120,000 − 91,575 = $28,425 Bond B (Premium): Total cash paid = 5 × $6,000 = $30,000 (coupon) + $100,000 (face at maturity) = $130,000 Total received at issuance = $108,904 Total Interest Expense = 130,000 − 108,904 = $21,096
Shortcut: Total Interest Expense = Total Cash Paid − Proceeds. EIM just allocates that total across periods using time-value logic. Bond A pays less per period but has higher total expense (started at a discount → paid that discount back via higher expense).
PART 5 — 5 Common Mistakes + 6 Key Takeaways

5 Common Mistakes

  1. Mixing up period rate vs annual rate.
    Semiannual → divide BOTH rates by 2, multiply periods by 2. Forgetting this is the #1 calculation killer.
  2. Using Face instead of Carrying Value for Interest Expense.
    Cash Interest uses Face. Interest Expense uses Carrying Value. Never swap these.
  3. Wrong amortization sign (subtracting for discount, adding for premium).
    Discount: CV + amortization each period. Premium: CV − amortization. If CV moves AWAY from Face, you have the sign wrong.
  4. PV formula arithmetic errors (especially the annuity factor).
    Write out: PVA factor = [1 − (1+r)^−n] / r. Compute step by step. Don't skip the denominator.
  5. Early retirement: forgetting to remove the remaining premium/discount.
    At retirement, debit Bonds Payable (face) AND the remaining premium/discount account. Then credit cash (call price). The plug is Gain/Loss.

6 Key Takeaways

  1. Discount: Stated < Market. Issue below face. CV walks up. Interest Expense > Cash paid. Debit Discount account at issuance, credit it each period.
  2. Premium: Stated > Market. Issue above face. CV walks down. Cash paid > Interest Expense. Credit Premium account at issuance, debit it each period.
  3. EIM is preferred by GAAP/IFRS because it produces a constant effective yield. Straight-line is simpler but less accurate (only allowed if immaterial).
  4. CV always reaches Face at maturity. If your final CV doesn't equal Face, check your arithmetic.
  5. Total Interest Expense = Total Cash Paid − Issue Proceeds. EIM just allocates it correctly across each period.
  6. Early Retirement Gain = CV > Call Price. Loss = CV < Call Price. Remove the bond (face + remaining premium/discount) from books; recognize the difference.
